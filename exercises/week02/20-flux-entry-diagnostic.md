## Questions

Four short closed-book questions at the start of the session. Questions 1–2 recall W2D1 operators; questions 3–4 probe existing flux intuition before teaching.

**Q1 (recall).** What direction does the gradient $\nabla u$ point, and what does its magnitude mean?

**Q2 (recall).** If $\nabla\times\vec{F}\neq\vec{0}$ locally, what does that imply about the field? If $\nabla\cdot\vec{F}>0$ locally, what does that imply?

**Q3 (pre-diagnostic).** Give a physical example of flux and explain in plain language what flux counts.

**Q4 (pre-diagnostic).** A uniform field $E=2~\text{V/m}$ passes perpendicularly through an area $A=5~\text{m}^2$. Compute the flux through the surface (choose a sensible name and unit).

## Key Results

- Q1: Gradient points in the direction of fastest growth of $u$; its magnitude is the rate of growth in that direction.
- Q2: Nonzero curl means local rotation (paddle-wheel test); positive divergence means a local source (net outflow).
- Q3: Magnetic flux through a loop: flux = (number of field lines per unit area) × area, counting how many field lines cross the surface. Valid intuition.
- Q4: $\Phi=EA=2\times 5=10~\text{V·m}$ (equivalently $10~\text{N·m}^2/\text{C}$).

## Feedback Summary

- **Q1:** Correct.
- **Q2:** Correct (rotation; source).
- **Q3:** Correct. The magnetic-field-line-count intuition is exactly the model adopted for the day.
- **Q4:** Basically correct. The arithmetic and unit intuition are right (answer 10). Small notation issue: initially used $\Psi$ and briefly $\varphi$, then the formula was rewritten to use $EA$. Standard notation for flux is uppercase $\Phi$ ($\phi$ and $\varphi$ are reserved for electric potential / phase). Unit V·m vs N·m²/C is not an error — they are dimensionally identical.
